An example house style for a fictional advisory firm, ACME, written for teaching. It sets out the presentation standards that Redline checks. The firm, its clients and every figure in the examples are invented.

# Requirements by PowerPoint Element

## Title Slides

- Typeface: Calibri (Headings)
- Size: 24 pt
- Show a valid date. Remove "DRAFT" and any similar status label
- Name the authors

## Section Header Slides

- Typeface: Calibri (Headings)
- Size: 24 pt

## Content Slides

A content slide combines one or more of:

- Bulleted text
- Data tables
- Charts and other visuals
- Groups of PowerPoint shapes

### Overall

- Keep the headline visibly separate from the body

### Slide Title (Headline)

- Typeface: Calibri Bold
- Size: 24 pt
- Case: Sentence case
- Punctuation: None at the end
- Placement: Vertically centred in the title box
- Size: Matches the "Title and Content" master
- Position: Matches the "Title and Content" master
- Content: State the conclusion, not only the subject
- Length: Warn when a headline runs past 3 lines

### Confidentiality Statement

- Font: Calibri (Body)
- Size: 8 pt
- Position: Matches the "Title and Content" master
- Use the legal wording exactly as written; never edit or paraphrase it
- Include it on every slide, **title and section header slides too**

## Content Slide Content

### Overall

- Warn when content sits closer to the slide's left edge than the headline's left edge
- Warn when content sits closer to the slide's right edge than the headline's right edge
- Keep everything below the headline at least 1" from the slide's left edge

### Bulleted Lists

- Typeface: Calibri (Body)
- Size of first-level bullets: 16 pt
- Size of lower-level bullets: 16 pt
- Bullet symbols:
  - Level 1: a square (∎ or ▪; either is accepted)
  - Level 2: a dash or en-dash (–)
  - Level 3: a right-pointing arrowhead (➢)
  - Levels 4–5: a round bullet (•)
- Alignment and indentation: left-aligned, indented the same way on every slide
- Line spacing: single
- Space before each Level 1 bullet: 6 pt
- Space before Level 2 to Level 5 bullets: 0 pt
- Indent of Level 2 bullets: .4"
- Indent of Level 3 to Level 5 bullets: .6"
- Punctuation: no full stop or other mark at the end of a bullet
- Writing: keep the bullets in one list grammatically parallel

### Tables

#### Overall

- Typeface: Calibri
- Size: 10–12 pt, the same throughout the table
- Borders: none on the table's left or right side
- Visual design
  - Keep gridlines light, or leave them out
  - Choose clarity over packing in data
  - Use shading and colour only where they carry meaning
- Number formatting:
  - The same number of decimal places within a table
  - Percentages always carry the % sign
  - Currency values always carry a currency symbol
  - Show missing or not-applicable values as an en-dash or a blank
- Write percentiles as "%ile", not "Percentile"

#### Table Title

- Typeface: Calibri (Body) Bold
- Size: 14 pt
- Spacing between lines: 0 pt
- Position: Centred above the table

#### Table Header

- Typeface: Bold
- Alignment: Aligned to the bottom of the cell
- Font colour: White
- Shading: #006EBE
- Borders: A thin black line above and below; no vertical border on the left or right
- Column groups: thin white lines inside the header may separate groups of columns, with a narrow spacer column between groups
- Structure: May run to more than one line
- Headers in tables of people
  - Label the column of names "Executive", not "Incumbent"
  - Label the column of job titles "Title" or "Position"
- *Informative, not enforced.* Where the column headers don't give units, a units label such as ($000s), in italics, may sit in the header's top-left cell, especially in pay tables. *Not checked yet: a candidate rule for a later release.*
- No border on the table's left or right side

#### Table Body

- Shading: Zebra stripes. The first body row has no fill; rows then alternate with #F2F2F2 (White, Background 1)
- Alignment: The same within each column

#### Statistics Row(s)

- A statistics row summarises the data above it, for example the 25th %ile, median and 75th %ile
- A table may have no statistics row
- Borders: A thin black line above and below; no vertical border on the left or right
- Shading: #FFFFDB (Yellow, Accent 3)
- *Informative, not enforced.* Statistics rows usually sit above the client row

#### Client Row(s)

- The client row is the row naming the client
- A table may have no client row
- Borders: A thin black line above and below; no vertical border on the left or right
- Shading: #F1FFE1 (Light Green, Accent 2)

### Charts and Visuals

#### Chart Title

- Typeface: Calibri (Body) Bold
- Size: 14 pt
- Spacing between lines: 0 pt
- Position: Centred above the chart or visual

#### Content

- Source: an image made in Excel or another tool, not built from PowerPoint shapes
- Label axes and legends clearly
- Colours: only from the ACME palette
- Paste tables as native PowerPoint tables, never as pictures

### Collections of PowerPoint Shapes

- Typeface: Calibri (Body)
- Size: The same across every shape
- Colours: From the ACME palette

### Footnotes & Sources

- Typeface: Calibri (Body) Italic
- Size: 10 pt
- Every footnote marker on a slide, in text, tables or graphics, needs a matching footnote at the bottom of that slide; no orphaned markers

### Chart Source Notes

- Typeface: Calibri (Body) Italic
- Size: 9 pt
- Placement: Directly beneath the chart or visual

> A chart source note is not a slide footnote. Footnotes are 10 pt; chart source notes are 9 pt.

### Emphasis and Annotations

- A red box draws the eye to one piece of information. Whatever the box highlights, the headline should mention too

## Writing Style & Terminology

- A professional tone, fit for a board
- Headlines that make a point, stated plainly
- Little jargon; standard pay terminology where needed
- The same terms on every slide and in every deck
- Say what it means, not just what happened

## Other

- Write "Target" in full, never "TGT"

# Appendix A: Do / Don't Examples for Presentation Standards

Worked examples of the standards above. Like many real style guides, a few of these examples disagree with the written requirements; DECISIONS.md explains how Redline resolves each conflict.

## Slide Title Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Title: "Bonus targets rise 10% to match the peer median" • Calibri Bold, 24 pt • Title box aligned to the bottom • No closing punctuation • States the conclusion | Title: "Bonus Plan Review" • Names only the subject • Centred • Ends with a full stop • Needs a speaker to explain it |

## Bullet Structure Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| • Bonus targets were raised to match the peer median -- Peers increased targets by 10% on average -- Keeps total pay competitive for key roles • Two bullet levels at most • Parallel grammar | • Bonus targets were raised. • Peers had put their targets up which meant ours were behind, so the committee agreed to change them. • Mixed sentence styles • Too much text |

## Table Formatting Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Bold header row. Measure \| Threshold \| Target \| Maximum. Operating margin \| 12.0% \| 14.0–15.0% \| >16.5% • Numbers right-aligned • No shading • Percentages formatted the same way | Heavy gridlines. A shaded header row. Different decimal places in one column. Numbers centred. |

## Color Usage Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| • Black body text • A blue accent on the key figure • Yellow highlight only on figures that changed | Several accent colours. Colour used as decoration. Red text where nothing is at risk. Yellow highlight used just for emphasis. |

## Chart Design Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| • Title left-aligned above the chart • The main series in blue, others in grey • Few gridlines • Source noted beneath the chart in 9 pt | A centred chart title. Many bright colours. 3D effects. No source. |

## Emphasis & Annotation Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Bold for the key conclusion. Strikethrough only for superseded targets. Yellow highlight only where the committee must decide. | Whole paragraphs underlined. Italics for emphasis. Several highlight colours. |

## Spacing & Density Example

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| • Plenty of white space • About 6 pt between bullets • The headline clearly apart from the body | A crowded slide. Bullets packed tight. Content pushed to the edges. |
